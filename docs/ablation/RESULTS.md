## Definitions

Each cell is one condition trained on one seed's dataset and evaluated on that dataset's held-out split of 105,000 error patterns. Percentages are rates over those episodes, in points. Conditions are aggregated over the 5 seeds with the mean, the sample standard deviation $s$ and a 95% confidence interval from the $t$ distribution with 4 degrees of freedom, $t_{0.975,4} = 2.776$, so the half-width written after ± is $1.24\,s$. Contrasts are differences within a seed, aggregated the same way, so seed-to-seed offsets cancel. No cell is bolded.

- **Success rate**: episodes whose terminal reason starts with `SUCCESS`, that is the agent chose STOP on the transmitted codeword.
- **Frame error rate (FER)**: episodes whose final word, after every flip the agent made, differs from the transmitted codeword. It is slightly below 100 minus the success rate, because a run can reach the correct word on its last permitted step without stopping.
- **Final BER**: bits still wrong at the end of the episode, over all bits of all held-out frames, in percent to three decimals.
- **Mean steps**: environment steps per episode, the STOP action included, with a limit of 12.
- **Weight**: number of flipped bits in the error pattern. Held-out patterns run from weight 0 to 6, the correction radius of the code.
- **Conditions**: `NoC_NoE` neither mechanism, `OnlyC` curriculum only, `OnlyE` expert seeding only, `Full` both. `OnlyC` and `Full` train and evaluate on the seed's curriculum dataset, `NoC_NoE` and `OnlyE` on the seed's flat dataset with the same marginal mixture, so baselines are identical within each pair and differ slightly between the pairs.

## 9. Training budget: multi-epoch conditions

A multi-epoch condition is a one-epoch condition trained for more passes over the same training words, each epoch restarting the phase schedule and reopening epsilon on a halving ladder (0.5, 0.25, 0.125, each annealing to 0.01 at the phase midpoint). Its weights are saved at every epoch boundary and every checkpoint is scored on the same held-out split as the one-epoch cell. Epoch 1 is read from the one-epoch cell, whose network it equals tensor for tensor. Increments are within-seed differences in points, aggregated like the contrasts in section 3. Success rates here carry two decimals because the increments are of the order of one point.
